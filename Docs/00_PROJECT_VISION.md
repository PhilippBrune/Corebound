# PROJECT VISION

## 1. Project Identity

**Working Title:** COREBOUND

**Genre:**

Action Roguelite × Deckbuilder × Tower Defense × Factory

**Platform:** Windows PC

**Distribution:** Steam

**Release Strategy:** Early Access

**Player Mode:** Singleplayer

**Engine:** Unity

**Perspective:** 2D or 2.5D, viewed primarily from above

**Input:** Controller First, with full Mouse and Keyboard support

**Development Model:** Solo development with AI-assisted design, programming, art, testing, balancing, and documentation.

**Initial Development Target:** A playable Early Access foundation within approximately six months of full-time development.

The working title, visual style, exact scope, and individual gameplay values may change during development. The core player experience and the combination of gameplay systems are the primary focus.

---

# 2. The Original Motivation

The project exists for two reasons:

1. To create a commercially viable, replayable PC game that combines several personally appealing genres into a coherent experience.
2. To explore what can be achieved when a solo developer collaborates with multiple AI models and AI agents throughout the game development process.

The game should not merely be a collection of AI-generated features. It should be a cohesive game with intentional design, satisfying gameplay, a recognizable identity, and a foundation that can be expanded through Early Access.

AI is a development tool. The quality of the player experience remains the primary measure of success.

---

# 3. High Concept

The player controls a combat-capable character who must protect a central energy core from increasingly dangerous enemy attacks.

The player fights directly, explores the arena, collects resources, constructs and upgrades defensive towers, and develops a powerful build through a card-based system.

The towers are not only defensive weapons. They can also become part of a production network that generates resources and supports further development.

Every run presents new opportunities and difficult decisions:

* Should the player fight or collect resources?
* Should resources be invested in towers or saved for a powerful card?
* Should the player strengthen their own weapons or improve the production network?
* Should the player choose a safe upgrade or a risky, high-impact synergy?
* Should the player build a specialized strategy or remain flexible?

The long-term objective is to create increasingly powerful and creative combinations of player weapons, cards, towers, and production systems while surviving escalating waves and defeating bosses.

> **COREBOUND is an action-focused roguelite in which the player fights, builds, and engineers a powerful defensive machine — one card, one tower, and one decision at a time.**

---

# 4. The Intended Player Experience

The player should feel like a combination of:

* A skilled combatant who actively influences every battle.
* A strategist who must make meaningful decisions under pressure.
* A builder who creates and improves a defensive network.
* An engineer who discovers resource-producing combinations.
* A deckbuilder who creates a unique and increasingly powerful build.
* An explorer who looks for opportunities, resources, and better positioning.

The game should produce the feeling:

> "My build is becoming stronger, my defensive network is coming alive, and I have a clever plan for how to survive the next challenge."

The player should regularly experience:

* The excitement of discovering a powerful card.
* The satisfaction of seeing two or more systems interact.
* The pressure of an approaching enemy wave.
* The reward of making a good investment.
* The surprise of an unexpected build synergy.
* The satisfaction of turning a weak early build into a powerful late-run machine.
* The desire to start another run and try a different strategy.

# 5. Core Gameplay Pillars

## 5.1 Action Comes First

The player is an active participant in combat.

The player must move, aim, attack, dodge or reposition, prioritize targets, and react to threats.

Towers and factory systems support the player, but they should not completely replace direct combat.

A player who makes good combat decisions should be able to meaningfully influence the outcome of a battle.

---

## 5.2 Build Creation and Synergy

The player develops a unique build during every run.

A build may focus on:

* Fast projectiles.
* Explosions and area damage.
* Critical hits.
* Elemental effects.
* Defensive towers.
* Resource production.
* Summoned units.
* Crowd control.
* High-risk, high-damage combinations.
* Hybrid strategies.

Individual cards and upgrades should be understandable on their own, but their greatest potential should emerge through combinations.

The game should reward experimentation rather than forcing one optimal strategy.

---

## 5.3 Meaningful Decisions

The player should regularly make decisions that affect the current run.

Examples:

* Improve the player or improve a tower?
* Build a new production tower or upgrade an existing one?
* Spend resources immediately or save for a legendary card?
* Choose a reliable upgrade or gamble on a powerful synergy?
* Invest in offense or strengthen the Core's defense?
* Explore for additional resources or prepare for the next wave?

Decisions should have consequences. The player should not always be able to obtain every available reward.

---

## 5.4 The Factory Is Part of the Build

The Factory system is not intended to become a separate, complex automation simulator like Factorio.

It is a focused gameplay system that allows the player to create production chains during a run.

For example:

Resource-producing tower
→ Generates resources
→ Resources fund new towers or card purchases
→ Cards improve production or combat
→ Improved systems generate greater value

The factory should create interesting investment decisions and synergies without overwhelming the action gameplay.

---

## 5.5 Readability and Player Control

Combat must remain understandable even when many enemies, projectiles, towers, and effects are present.

The player must be able to recognize:

* Their own position.
* Enemy positions and threats.
* The Core's condition.
* Tower locations and functions.
* Available resources.
* Important projectiles and area effects.
* The current wave and upcoming danger.

Visual spectacle must support gameplay, not obscure it.

---

## 5.6 Replayability

The game should be designed for repeated runs.

Each run should offer different combinations of:

* Weapons.
* Cards.
* Towers.
* Resources.
* Upgrades.
* Enemy compositions.
* Events or challenges.
* Boss encounters.

The player should be able to pursue different strategies and discover new interactions over time.

---

# 6. The Core Gameplay Loop

The intended loop combines real-time action, resource collection, construction, card-based progression, and escalating defense.

## 6.1 During a Wave

1. The player moves through the arena.
2. Enemies approach the Core or attack the player.
3. The player fights directly.
4. Enemies drop or generate resources.
5. The player collects resources and evaluates the battlefield.
6. Existing towers attack, defend, or produce resources.
7. The player decides whether to reposition, construct, upgrade, or continue fighting.
8. The player survives the wave or suffers a critical failure.

## 6.2 Between Waves

1. The immediate combat pressure decreases.
2. The player reviews resources and the current build.
3. The player selects cards, upgrades, or other rewards.
4. The player constructs or upgrades towers.
5. The player considers saving resources for a more powerful purchase.
6. The player prepares for the next wave.

The exact timing of building, card selection, and purchasing remains subject to the dedicated gameplay and round specifications.

## 6.3 During a Run

The player progresses through a series of increasingly difficult stages.

A possible structure is:

Start Run
→ Initial equipment and build
→ Early waves
→ Card and resource decisions
→ Stronger enemy combinations
→ Elite encounters or special challenges
→ Stage Boss
→ New stage or environment
→ Further build development
→ Final challenge
→ Run victory or defeat

This structure is a design target, not yet a finalized rule set.

# 7. The Three Core Systems

## 7.1 Deckbuilder

Cards are the primary mechanism for developing the player's build during a run.

Cards may modify:

* Player weapons.
* Projectile behavior.
* Damage.
* Attack speed.
* Critical hits.
* Elemental effects.
* Tower behavior.
* Tower production.
* Resource generation.
* Resource costs.
* Defensive systems.
* Movement.
* Risk and reward mechanics.

Cards should create combinations that change how the player approaches the game.

### Example

A player may combine:

* A card that makes projectiles split.
* A card that causes projectiles to explode.
* A card that increases explosion damage.
* A card that causes explosions to generate resources.

The result is not merely four separate bonuses. Together, they create a new strategic direction.

The exact card acquisition method, deck rules, stacking behavior, rarity system, and card pool will be defined in the card specifications.

---

## 7.2 Tower Defense

The player constructs defensive towers around the Core or in designated buildable areas.

Towers may:

* Attack enemies.
* Slow or control enemies.
* Protect the Core.
* Support the player.
* Generate resources.
* Enhance other towers.
* Create production chains.
* Interact with cards and other towers.

Towers should have different strategic purposes rather than being simple variations of damage output.

The player should need to consider:

* Placement.
* Range.
* Target priority.
* Construction cost.
* Upgrade cost.
* Defensive coverage.
* Production value.
* Synergies.
* The current enemy composition.

The initial playable version should contain approximately three meaningful tower types.

---

## 7.3 Factory

The Factory system allows the player to turn defensive infrastructure into an economic advantage.

The player should be able to create focused production chains, such as:

* Resource generation.
* Resource amplification.
* Resource conversion.
* Tower-supported production.
* Production-based card synergies.

The Factory should answer a strategic question:

> "Can I invest resources now to create a stronger economy that will help me survive later?"

Factory mechanics must remain subordinate to the action and defense experience.

The system should not require the player to manage dozens of independent machines, complex logistics networks, or a separate factory-building interface.

---

# 8. Combat and Player Character

The player controls a combat-capable character in a 2D or 2.5D arena.

The character should support:

* Responsive movement.
* Direct attacks.
* A small set of clearly differentiated weapons.
* Positioning and target prioritization.
* Defensive or evasive movement.
* Interaction with resources and towers.
* Build-based combat modifications.

The initial playable version should contain two or three distinct weapons.

Possible weapon design directions include:

* A rapid-fire projectile weapon.
* A powerful slow-firing weapon.
* A close-range or area-damage weapon.
* A weapon that interacts strongly with the Factory.
* A weapon that specializes in elemental or status effects.

The final weapons must be defined by their gameplay roles and interactions, not merely by different damage values.

# 9. Initial Content Target

The first playable Early Access foundation should aim to contain:

## Player

* One playable character.
* Responsive movement.
* Controller support.
* Mouse and Keyboard support.
* Basic combat.
* A defined health and damage system.

## Weapons

* Two to three distinct weapons.
* Clear differences in playstyle.
* Card-based modification support.

## Towers

* Approximately three tower types.
* Distinct defensive or production roles.
* Construction and upgrade functionality.
* Interaction with cards and resources.

## Cards

* Approximately 30 initial cards.
* Multiple rarities or power levels, if appropriate.
* Meaningful synergies.
* A mix of player, weapon, tower, economy, and utility effects.
* At least some high-impact combinations.

## Enemies

* Five distinct enemy types.
* Clear differences in behavior and threat.
* Interactions with player combat and tower defense.

## Boss

* One complete boss encounter.
* Distinct attack patterns.
* Clear telegraphs.
* A meaningful challenge for different builds.
* A defined reward or progression outcome.

## Run

* A complete playable run structure.
* Waves and escalating difficulty.
* Resource collection.
* Construction and upgrades.
* Card acquisition.
* A clear victory and defeat condition.

## Supporting Systems

* Main menu.
* Pause menu.
* Settings.
* Save and load functionality, where required.
* Basic meta progression.
* HUD and relevant gameplay interfaces.
* Audio and visual feedback.
* Steam-ready Windows build pipeline.

These are initial scope targets. Quality, coherence, and fun take priority over reaching a particular content count.

# 10. Run Duration and Progression

## Intended Run Duration

Approximately 60–70 minutes for a complete run.

The game should provide meaningful progression throughout the run rather than concentrating all important decisions at the beginning or end.

The exact number of waves, stages, shops, elite encounters, and bosses remains to be specified.

## In-Run Progression

During a run, the player may gain:

* New cards.
* Weapon improvements.
* Tower improvements.
* New tower access.
* Resources.
* Temporary power increases.
* Build-defining effects.
* Special rewards.

## Meta Progression

Between runs, the player may unlock:

* New cards.
* New weapons.
* New tower types.
* New starting options.
* New challenges.
* New bosses or stages.
* New build possibilities.

Meta progression should expand the player's options and encourage experimentation.

It should not completely replace player skill or make every subsequent run automatically easy.

# 11. Long-Term Player Motivation

After approximately 100 hours, the game should still offer reasons to continue playing.

Potential sources of long-term motivation include:

* Discovering new builds.
* Completing card and weapon collections.
* Unlocking new towers and starting options.
* Mastering different playstyles.
* Higher difficulty levels.
* Challenging enemy combinations.
* Boss mastery.
* Alternative build objectives.
* High scores or performance records.
* Achievements.
* Special challenges.
* Finding unusual and powerful synergies.

The final endgame structure is not yet fixed. It should be designed around replayability and build experimentation rather than simply increasing enemy health.

# 12. Inspiration

The following games are reference points for specific experiences, not templates to copy.

## Portal

Inspiration:

* A strong central gameplay concept.
* Mechanics that can be used in creative ways.
* Clear rules with surprising interactions.
* The satisfaction of solving problems through experimentation.

## Brotato

Inspiration:

* Fast, readable combat.
* Short-term build decisions.
* Strong item and stat synergies.
* High replayability.

## Salt and Sanctuary

Inspiration:

* Exploration.
* Discovering new areas and opportunities.
* Character progression.
* The feeling of navigating a dangerous world.

The final game does not need to be a traditional Metroidvania. Exploration elements should only be included if they support the core action, defense, and resource systems.

## Hades 2

Inspiration:

* Responsive action.
* Distinctive weapons and abilities.
* Strong build development.
* Exciting encounters.
* Clear progression and replayability.
* High-quality feedback and presentation.

## Ball x Pit

Inspiration:

* Escalating combat.
* Build combinations.
* Defense and survival pressure.
* Strong progression loops.
* The satisfaction of increasingly powerful interactions.

The goal is to combine the most appealing experiences from these references into a distinct game.

# 13. Visual and Presentation Direction

The game should be designed primarily for 2D or 2.5D presentation.

The exact visual style is not yet finalized, but it should support the following goals.

## Desired Characteristics

* Strong visual identity.
* Clear silhouettes.
* Readable combat.
* Distinctive cards and icons.
* Recognizable towers.
* Clear enemy telegraphs.
* Satisfying projectile and impact effects.
* Attractive screenshots and gameplay videos for Steam.
* A style that can be produced and expanded efficiently with AI assistance.

## Possible Art Direction

A promising direction to investigate is:

**Stylized techno-fantasy.**

The world could combine:

* An ancient or mysterious energy Core.
* Industrial machinery.
* Crystalline or magical resources.
* Mechanical towers.
* Distinctive enemy designs.
* A visually striking arena.
* Strong contrast between the player's systems and enemy forces.

Alternative directions worth prototyping:

1. **Arcane Industrial:** Magic crystals, machinery, runes, and mechanical defenses.
2. **Dark Sci-Fi:** A failing energy Core, advanced technology, and hostile entities.
3. **Stylized Mechanical Fantasy:** Strong geometric shapes, expressive effects, and colorful construction systems.

The visual direction must be selected based on readability, production feasibility, and the game's identity.

It should not be finalized before visual prototypes and gameplay tests have been evaluated.

# 14. Controls and Accessibility

The game is designed for Windows PC with a Controller First approach.

## Controller

The player should be able to:

* Move.
* Aim or select targets.
* Attack.
* Use defensive abilities.
* Interact with resources.
* Build towers.
* Upgrade towers.
* Navigate menus.
* Select cards.
* Pause and resume the game.

## Mouse and Keyboard

The game must also support:

* Keyboard movement.
* Mouse aiming where appropriate.
* Keyboard or mouse combat actions.
* Menu navigation.
* Tower placement.
* Card selection.
* Rebinding, if included in the final input scope.

Controller support must not be treated as an afterthought.

The game should provide a clear and professional interface.

Expected main menu functions include:

* Start Run.
* Continue, if supported by the save design.
* Load Game, if supported.
* Collection or unlocks.

* UI development.
* Art and visual assets.
* Audio concepts.

* Specification checks.
* Code review.
* Compilation.
* Automated tests where appropriate.

The purpose of AI assistance is to accelerate development and explore new workflows, not to eliminate the need for design decisions or quality control.

# 18. What This Game Is Not

COREBOUND is not intended to be:

* A large open-world game.
* An MMORPG.
* A multiplayer game.
* A full-scale Factorio-style factory simulator.
* A traditional card game in which cards are primarily played one by one during combat.
* A passive tower defense game in which the player has little direct influence.
* A direct clone of Hades, Brotato, Ball x Pit, or another reference game.
* A project that sacrifices gameplay quality for a large number of features.
* A collection of unrelated mechanics.

The game should remain focused on its central combination:

**Action + Build Creation + Tower Defense + Focused Factory Systems.**

# 19. Design Boundaries

The following principles guide future design decisions:

1. The player must remain an active participant in combat.
2. The Factory must support the core game instead of becoming a separate simulator.
3. Cards must create meaningful build decisions and interactions.
4. Towers must have distinct strategic purposes.
5. Resources must create interesting choices rather than unnecessary busywork.
6. The game must remain readable during intense combat.
7. Controller support must be considered from the beginning.
8. New content should expand existing systems whenever possible.
9. A smaller, polished feature set is preferable to a larger, unfinished one.
10. The game should reward experimentation and build diversity.
11. The game should feel cohesive rather than like several genres placed next to each other.
12. Every major feature must justify its place in the player experience.

---

# 20. Open Design Questions

The following decisions must be resolved in dedicated specifications or design reviews:

* Is the game fully top-down 2D, 2.5D, or another camera arrangement?
* Is movement free in an arena, room-based, or exploration-based?
* How exactly does the player aim?
* Is the Core stationary or can it be upgraded or moved?
* How are buildable tower locations determined?
* How are resources collected?
* Which resource types are needed?
* How does the Factory generate and transport resources?
* Are cards selected from random offers, purchased, drafted, or acquired through rewards?
* How does the player's deck work?
* Are cards permanent for a run, stackable, consumable, or replaceable?
* How many towers can be active?
* How many weapons can the player use simultaneously?
* How are stages and waves structured?
* How does the boss system work?
* What is the precise meta progression?
* What is the final visual art direction?
* What content is essential for the first Early Access release?

These questions must be resolved before the affected systems are considered fully specified.

# 21. Definition of Vision Success

The vision is successfully represented in the game when a player can:

1. Enter a run and immediately understand the basic objective.
2. Fight enemies directly and feel that their actions matter.
3. Collect resources and make meaningful spending decisions.
4. Construct and improve defensive towers.
5. Develop a build through cards.
6. Discover useful interactions between combat, towers, cards, and production.
7. Experience increasing pressure and escalating power.
8. Defeat challenging encounters through skill and strategic decisions.
9. Finish a run with a build that feels distinct from previous runs.
10. Want to play another run to try a different strategy.

**The most important success criterion:**

The game should make the player think:

> "I have an idea for a new build. I want to try one more run."

# 22. Final Vision Statement

COREBOUND is a singleplayer Windows action roguelite for Steam that combines direct combat, deckbuilding, tower defense, and focused Factory mechanics.

The player protects a central Core, fights enemies, collects resources, constructs a defensive network, and develops increasingly powerful builds through cards and synergies.

Every run should be a journey from a vulnerable beginning to a powerful, personalized combat-and-production system.

The game should be easy to understand at its foundation, deep through its interactions, satisfying to control, and rewarding to replay.

The project also serves as an experiment in AI-assisted game development: a solo developer and multiple AI agents working together to create a complete, expandable, commercially presentable game.

**Build your strategy. Engineer your defense. Survive the Corebound.**
